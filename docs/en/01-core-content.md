## 1. Training Philosophy

| Principle | Description | Training Application |
|:----------|:------------|:--------------------|
| Movement Quality > Training Quantity | Perfect technique is the foundation of progress | Practice each movement slowly without load first; increase volume only after reaching standard |
| Neuromuscular Control > Strength Output | Establish correct movement patterns | Proprioception training prioritized over strength training |
| Injury Prevention > Performance Seeking | Prevention is better than cure | Always perform activation and stabilization exercises before each training session |
| Long-term Development > Short-term Progress | 3-year plan, not a 3-month plan | Do not rush; advance only after meeting standards at each Level |

### Module 1: Neuromuscular Control Foundation

#### 1.1 Foot Proprioception Training

**CSCS Scientific Basis:**
- The foot is the starting point of the kinetic chain; 90% of sports injuries originate from poor foot control
- Good foot control improves movement efficiency by 30%

| Exercise | Key Points | Training Volume | Progression |
|:---------|:-----------|:---------------:|:------------|
| Single-leg stance (eyes open) | Supporting foot flat on ground, knee slightly bent, core engaged | 30 sec × 3 sets each side | Progress to eyes closed |
| Single-leg stance (eyes closed) | Same as above, rely on proprioception after closing eyes | 20 sec × 3 sets each side | 30 sec without swaying = excellent |
| Plantar muscle activation | Seated, repeatedly grasp towel/floor with toes | 3 sets × 15 reps | Practice in standing position |
| Barefoot walking | Walk slowly barefoot on flat ground, feel the soles | 2 times/week × 15 min | Try different surfaces |

**Common Errors:**
- Excessive body sway → Core not engaged; tighten abdomen and try again
- Toes unable to move independently → Do toe separation exercises first (manual assistance)

#### 1.2 Core Stability Neural Control

**CSCS Scientific Basis:**
- The core is the center of force transmission; badminton requires 360-degree core control
- Core stability improves force transmission efficiency by 40%

| Exercise | Movement Breakdown | Training Volume | Progression Standard |
|:---------|:------------------|:---------------:|:--------------------|
| Dead Bug | ① Lie on back, arms and legs perpendicular to ground ② Lower back pressed to floor ③ Exhale, lower opposite arm and leg ④ Inhale, return | 3 sets × 10 reps each side | Slow down to 3-0-3 tempo |
| Bird Dog | ① Four-point kneeling ② Neutral spine ③ Raise opposite arm and leg simultaneously ④ Hold 2 sec, then lower | 3 sets × 8 reps each side | Lift leg on an unstable surface (pad) |
| Plank (Basic) | ① Forearms on ground ② Body in straight line ③ Core engaged ④ Breathe evenly | 3 sets × 30 sec | 60 sec → 90 sec |

**Common Errors:**
- Dead Bug: lower back lifts off ground → Reduce arm/leg lowering range; keep back on ground
- Bird Dog: body sways → Slow down; practice one side first

#### 1.3 Basic Footwork Neural Pathways

**CSCS Scientific Basis:**
- Badminton requires rapid direction changes; efficient footwork saves 25% energy consumption

| Exercise | Movement Breakdown | Training Volume | Progression |
|:---------|:------------------|:---------------:|:------------|
| Split Squat (no load) | ① Stand ② Step forward into lunge ③ Front knee at 90° ④ Back knee near ground ⑤ Push back to standing | 3 sets × 8 reps each side | Slow → Fast → Explosive |
| Lateral Shuffle | ① Athletic ready position ② Shuffle right 3 steps ③ Brake ④ Shuffle left back | 3 sets × 5 reps each direction | Add turn movement |
| Athletic Stance Hold | ① Feet wider than shoulder-width ② Knees bent ③ Lower center of gravity ④ Torso upright | Hold 30 sec × 3 sets | 60 sec |

**Common Errors:**
- Split squat: knee extends too far past toes → Take a longer stride
- Shuffle: body bobs up and down → Keep center of gravity height constant

#### 1.4 Upper Extremity Kinetic Chain Development

**CSCS Scientific Basis:**
- Badminton strokes involve full-body kinetic chain transfer; correct kinetic chain increases stroke power by 50%

| Exercise | Movement Breakdown | Training Volume | Key Points |
|:---------|:------------------|:---------------:|:-----------|
| Medicine Ball Throw (2-3 kg) | ① Sidestance ② Drive through legs and rotate ③ Core transfers ④ Upper limb releases ⑤ Throw against wall | 3 sets × 8 reps | Feel the force transfer from feet to hands |
| Resistance Band Clear Simulation | ① Anchor one end of band ② Simulate clear stroke ③ Scapular retraction → arm extension | 3 sets × 10 reps | Slow and controlled; feel the sequence of force generation |
| Scap Push-up | ① Plank position ② Retract shoulder blades ③ Protract | 3 sets × 12 reps | Arms stay straight; only move shoulder blades |

**Common Errors:**
- Medicine ball thrown with only the arm → Generate force from the legs first, then transfer
- Resistance band movement disconnected → Break down into: rotate → extend arm → generate power

### Module 2: Structural Strength Foundation

#### 2.1 Shoulder Joint Dynamic Stability

**CSCS Scientific Basis:** Badminton shoulder injury rate is 27%; shoulder stability improves stroke accuracy by 35%

| Exercise | Movement Breakdown | Training Volume | Progression |
|:---------|:------------------|:---------------:|:------------|
| Resistance Band External Rotation | ① Elbow pinned at side at 90° ② Hold band ③ Rotate forearm outward | 3 sets × 15 reps | Increase band resistance |
| Resistance Band Internal Rotation | ① Same as above ② Rotate forearm inward | 3 sets × 15 reps | Control speed |
| YTWL Exercise | ① Bending forward ② Perform Y/T/W/L shaped raises | 10 reps each × 2 sets | Add light weight |

**Common Errors:**
- Shrugging as compensation → Lower shoulders before performing
- Elbow moves away from body during rotation → Fix by holding a towel between elbow and body

#### 2.2 Knee Preventive Training

**CSCS Scientific Basis:** Badminton knee injury rate is 35%; knee stability reduces injury risk by 60%

| Exercise | Movement Breakdown | Training Volume | Progression |
|:---------|:------------------|:---------------:|:------------|
| Single-Leg Squat (Eccentric Control) | ① Stand on one leg ② Slow descent over 3 sec ③ Fast rise in 1 sec | 3 sets × 8 reps each side | Increase squat depth |
| Side-Lying Leg Raise | ① Lie on side ② Upper leg straight ③ Lift upward ④ Lower slowly | 3 sets × 15 reps each side | Add small ankle weight |
| Clamshell | ① Lie on side, knees bent ② Open knees like a clamshell ③ Lower with control | 3 sets × 15 reps each side | Place resistance band above knees |

**Common Errors:**
- Knee caves inward during squat → Align knee with second toe
- Torso rotates during clamshell → Keep pelvis fixed and still

#### 2.3 Basic Strength Endurance

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Key Points |
|:---------|:-----------:|:-----------|
| Bodyweight Squat | 3 × 20 | Knees not past toes excessively; thighs parallel to ground |
| Kneeling Push-up | 3 × 15 | Shoulder blades engaged; body in straight line on descent |
| Resistance Band Row | 3 × 20 | Scapular retraction drives the arms; hold last rep of each set for 3 sec |
| Glute Bridge | 3 × 15 | Lift by driving through glutes; hold peak contraction for 2 sec |

**Time Under Tension:** 3-4 seconds per repetition; maintain continuous muscular tension

### Module 3: Neuropsychological Training

#### 3.1 Visual Attention Training

**Top Coach Experience:** Badminton is a vision-dominant sport; visual training improves reaction speed by 30%

| Exercise | Method | Duration | Progression |
|:---------|:-------|:--------:|:------------|
| Multi-Target Tracking | Track 2-3 moving objects simultaneously (e.g., multiple shuttlecocks) | 5 min | Increase number or speed |
| Selective Attention | Focus on a specific target amidst noise/distractions | 5 min | Increase distraction intensity |
| Peripheral Vision | Look straight ahead; use peripheral vision to sense lateral movement | 5 min | Narrow visual focus while maintaining peripheral awareness |

#### 3.2 Decision Speed Training

| Exercise | Method | Duration | Progression |
|:---------|:-------|:--------:|:------------|
| Pattern Recognition | Observe coach's ready position; predict shuttlecock direction | 10 min | Add deceptive movement interference |
| Reaction Choice | Different color/direction signals trigger different movement responses | 10 min | Shorten reaction time |
| Random Signal | Random pointing; immediately execute corresponding footwork movement | 5 min | 3 consecutive correct = 1 set |

#### 3.3 Psychological Resilience Foundation

| Exercise | Method | Frequency | Progression |
|:---------|:-------|:---------:|:------------|
| Pressure Adaptation | Start with low-pressure environments; gradually add competitive pressure factors | Once/week | Add scoring/spectators/umpires |
| Self-Talk | Identify negative thoughts; convert to positive affirmations | Every training session | Establish 3 key words (e.g., "Focus", "Relax", "Explode") |
| Attention Focus Control | Switch between "process focus" and "outcome focus" | 5 min each training session | Rapid switching drills |

#### 4.2 Structural Recovery

**Fascial Release:**
- **Tools**: Foam roller (large muscle groups), massage ball (small muscles / soles of feet)
- **Frequency**: 5-10 minutes daily
- **Key Areas**: Soles of feet → Posterior calves → Anterior thighs → Glutes → Shoulders
- **Method**: Find tender point; hold for 30 sec with slow breathing; then move slowly

**Fascial Hydration:**

| Period | Water Requirement | Electrolyte Supplement |
|:-------|:------------------|:-----------------------|
| Daily | Body weight (kg) × 40 ml/day | — |
| Pre-training | 500 ml, 2 hours before training | — |
| During training | 150-200 ml every 15 min | Sports drink / electrolyte water |
| Post-training | Body weight lost × 1.5 times | Supplement sodium, potassium, magnesium |
