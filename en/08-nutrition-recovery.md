# Badminton Nutrition & Recovery — Principles and Calculation Methods

> **For whom**: Coaches and athletes themselves
> **Goal**: After reading this, you can calculate for yourself what to eat, how much, and when — based on your own body weight, body fat percentage, training volume, and training goals
> **Core philosophy**: A nutrition plan is not a recipe book — it's formulas + principles. Learn to calculate, and it serves you for life.

## Chapter 1: Energy — Start with the Numbers

### 1.1 Calculating TDEE

The first step of any nutrition plan is figuring out exactly how many calories you burn in a day.

**Step 1: Calculate BMR**

Formula (Mifflin-St Jeor, currently the most accurate):

```
Male:   BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age + 5
Female: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age - 161
```

**Example:** 70kg male, 175cm, 25 years old
BMR = 10×70 + 6.25×175 - 5×25 + 5 = 700 + 1094 - 125 + 5 = **1674 kcal/day**

**Step 2: Multiply by activity factor to get TDEE**

| Activity Level | Description | Factor | Who It Applies To |
|:---------------|:------------|:------:|:------------------|
| Sedentary | Barely any exercise | 1.2 | Injury recovery |
| Light | 1-3 low-intensity sessions/week | 1.375 | Level 0-1 off days |
| Moderate | 3-5 moderate-intensity sessions/week | 1.55 | Level 0-1 training days |
| High | 6-7 high-intensity sessions/week | 1.725 | Level 2+ |
| Very High | 2 sessions/day or physical labor | 1.9 | Professional athletes |

**Continuing the example:** Moderate activity (1.55)
TDEE = 1674 × 1.55 = **2595 kcal/day**

### 1.2 Adjusting Energy Intake by Goal

| Goal | Calorie Adjustment | Notes |
|:-----|:-------------------|:------|
| Maintain weight | TDEE × 1.0 | Maintain current weight |
| Fat loss (slow) | TDEE - 300kcal | ~0.25kg/week loss, suitable during season |
| Fat loss (fast) | TDEE - 500kcal | ~0.5kg/week loss, suitable off-season |
| Muscle gain (slow) | TDEE + 200kcal | Clean bulking, suitable during season |
| Muscle gain (fast) | TDEE + 500kcal | Suitable off-season / adolescent growth |

**⚠️ Fat loss floor:**
- Men: no lower than 1500 kcal/day
- Women: no lower than 1200 kcal/day
- Below these thresholds, metabolic adaptation severely reduces results

## Chapter 2: The Three Macronutrients — Calculated by Body Weight

### 2.1 Protein: The Simplest and Most Important

**Formula:**

| Goal | Protein g/kg bodyweight/day | Daily need for 70kg person |
|:-----|:---------------------------:|:--------------------------:|
| General maintenance | 1.2-1.6 g/kg | 84-112g |
| Muscle gain | 1.6-2.0 g/kg | 112-140g |
| Fat loss (preserve muscle) | 2.0-2.4 g/kg | 140-168g |
| Adolescent athletes | 1.8-2.2 g/kg | 126-154g |

**Distribution principles (don't eat it all at once):**
- 20-40g protein per meal (optimal range for muscle protein synthesis per meal)
- 20-30g fast-absorbing protein within 30 minutes post-training
- 20-30g slow protein before bed (casein / yogurt)

**How to calculate protein in food:**
```
100g chicken breast  ≈ 31g protein
1 whole egg         ≈ 6-7g protein
100g salmon         ≈ 20g protein
100g tofu           ≈ 8g protein
100g Greek yogurt   ≈ 10g protein
1 scoop whey protein ≈ 25g protein
```

**Calculate your own numbers:**
```
Your body weight: ____ kg
Your goal: ____ (maintain / gain / lose)
Protein per kg: ____ g
Total daily protein: ____ × ____ = ____ g
Over ____ meals: ~____ g per meal
```

### 2.2 Fat: Not the Enemy — It's About Type

**Formula:**

| Goal | Fat g/kg bodyweight/day | % of calories | Daily for 70kg |
|:-----|:-----------------------:|:-------------:|:--------------:|
| General | 0.8-1.0 g/kg | 20-25% | 56-70g |
| Muscle gain | 0.8-1.2 g/kg | 20-30% | 56-84g |
| Fat loss | 0.6-0.8 g/kg | 15-20% | 42-56g |

**Fat type ratios:**
```
Saturated fat (animal fats, coconut oil):          ≤10% of total calories
Monounsaturated fat (olive oil, avocado):          ≥10% of total calories
Polyunsaturated fat (Omega-3/6, fish oil):         ≥10% of total calories
Trans fat:                                          as close to 0 as possible
```

**How to tell if you're deficient in fat:**
- Dry skin, increased joint noise
- Irregular menstrual cycles (women)
- Slower training recovery
- Difficulty concentrating

### 2.3 Carbohydrates: The Most Important Training Fuel

**Formula:**

| Training Intensity | Carbs g/kg bodyweight/day | Daily for 70kg | Applicable Phase |
|:------------------|:-------------------------:|:--------------:|:-----------------|
| Low intensity / rest day | 2-3 g/kg | 140-210g | Level 0 rest day |
| Moderate intensity training day | 3-5 g/kg | 210-350g | Level 0-1 training day |
| High intensity training day | 5-7 g/kg | 350-490g | Level 2+ |
| Very high intensity / 2 sessions | 7-10 g/kg | 490-700g | Professional athletes |

**Carb type selection (by GI value / absorption speed):**

| Timing | Carb Type | GI | Examples |
|:-------|:----------|:--:|:---------|
| 2-3 hours before training | Low GI | <55 | Oats, whole-wheat bread, sweet potato |
| 30 minutes before training | Moderate GI | 55-70 | Banana, whole-wheat crackers |
| During training (>60 min) | High GI / fast replenishment | >70 | Sports drink, glucose gel |
| Within 30 minutes post-training | High GI | >70 | White rice, potato, honey |
| 1-2 hours post-training | Moderate GI | 55-70 | Whole-wheat pasta, brown rice |

**⚠️ Key principles (many people get this backwards):**
- **Before training**: Low GI, provides steady energy
- **After training**: High GI, rapidly replenishes muscle glycogen
- **Before bed**: Low GI or none, avoid blood sugar spikes that interfere with growth hormone

### 2.4 Putting It Together: Calculate Your Own Macronutrient Split

```
【Your Personal Info】
Body weight: ____ kg
Training day / Rest day: ____
Goal: ____

Step 1: Calculate total calories
TDEE = ____ kcal
Adjusted calories = ____ kcal

Step 2: Set protein first (most strict)
Protein = body weight × ____ g/kg = ____ g
Calories from protein = ____ × 4 = ____ kcal

Step 3: Set fat second (second most strict)
Fat = body weight × ____ g/kg = ____ g
Calories from fat = ____ × 9 = ____ kcal

Step 4: Remaining calories go to carbs
Calories from carbs = total - protein cals - fat cals = ____ kcal
Carbs = ____ ÷ 4 = ____ g
Carbs ÷ body weight = ____ g/kg ✅ Check if within reasonable range

【Result】Training day:
Protein ____g · Fat ____g · Carbs ____g
Total calories ____ kcal
```

## Chapter 3: Strategy Differences by Body Type

> ⚠️ **Note**: The somatotype classification (ectomorph/mesomorph/endomorph) originated from Sheldon (1940s).
> Modern sports nutrition guidelines (ACSM, ISSN) recommend basing nutrition prescriptions on measured data
> (body weight, body fat %, training volume) rather than body type classification.
> **Provided here as rough reference only** — your actual plan should be adjusted based on your body's response.

### 3.1 The Three Body Types (Reference)

| Body Type | Characteristics | Metabolic Traits | Nutritional Strategy Focus |
|:----------|:---------------|:-----------------|:--------------------------|
| **Ectomorph** (lean-built) | Small frame, low body fat, hard to gain weight | Fast metabolism, high calorie burn | Higher carb ratio (total+), total cals +300 |
| **Mesomorph** (athletic) | Moderate frame, easy to build muscle, easy to lose fat | Normal metabolism | Standard formula works |
| **Endomorph** (full-built) | Large frame, stores fat easily, muscle gains not obvious | Slow metabolism, carb-sensitive | Lower carb ratio, control GI, total cals -200 |

**How to tell:** Observe your "natural state"
- You were lean as a young person without much exercise → ectomorph
- Muscle definition appears quickly when you train → mesomorph
- You gain weight easily even without overeating, and lose weight but rebound → endomorph

### 3.2 Body Type Adjustment Factors

| Adjustment | Ectomorph | Mesomorph | Endomorph |
|:-----------|:---------:|:---------:|:---------:|
| Total calorie adjustment | +15-20% | 0% | -10% |
| Carb ratio | 60% → increase | 50-55% | 40-45% |
| Fat ratio | 20% | 25% | 30-35% |
| Protein ratio | 20% | 20-25% | 25-30% |
| Carb selection | Low GI primarily, occasional high GI | By timing | Strictly low GI |

## Chapter 4: Nutrition Adjustments Across Training Cycles

### 4.1 Training Days vs Rest Days

You shouldn't eat the same way on both. This is the biggest mistake.

| Item | Training Day | Rest Day |
|:-----|:-------------|:---------|
| Total calories | TDEE + consumption | TDEE × 1.2 (sedentary factor) |
| Carbs | Upper end of formula | Lower end of formula (2g/kg is enough) |
| Protein | Middle of formula | Lower end of formula |
| Fat | Lower end of formula | Upper end of formula |

**Principle: More carbs on training days, more fat on rest days. Protein stays the same.**

### 4.2 Deload Week Nutrition Adjustments

Deload week reduces training volume by 50%; nutrition should adjust accordingly:

```
Carbs:   Reduce by 30% (less training means less fuel needed)
Protein: Keep the same (repair still needs to happen)
Fat:     Slightly increase (fill the calorie gap from reduced carbs)
Total calories: Reduce by 20% (because training expenditure is also down)
```

### 4.3 In-Season vs Off-Season

| Phase | Goal | Nutrition Strategy |
|:------|:-----|:-------------------|
| Off-season (bulking) | Increase lean mass | TDEE + 300-500, carbs at upper limit |
| Preparation (training) | Improve performance | TDEE + 0-200, carbs adjusted by training |
| In-season (competition) | Maintain weight + performance | TDEE + 0, carbs concentrated around matches |
| Transition (rest) | Recovery + fat loss | TDEE - 300, carbs at lower limit + high quality |

## Chapter 5: Nutrient Timing — Get It Wrong and You Might as Well Not Eat

### 5.1 Pre-Training Nutrition

| Time | Purpose | What to Eat | Formula |
|:-----|:--------|:------------|:--------|
| 2-3 hours before training | Main meal, sustained energy | Low GI carbs + moderate protein + low fat | Carbs 1-2g/kg + protein 0.3g/kg |
| 30-60 minutes before training | Snack, boost performance | Moderate GI carbs + small protein | Carbs 0.3-0.5g/kg |
| Within 10 minutes before training | Fast energy | Fast carbs (banana/glucose) | Carbs 0.1-0.2g/kg |

**Calculation: 70kg athlete, 2 hours before training**
- Carbs: 70 × 1.5 = 105g ≈ 1.5 bowls of oats + 1 banana
- Protein: 70 × 0.3 = 21g ≈ 1 scoop whey protein or 3 egg whites
- Fat: keep minimal (fat digests slowly and drags down performance)

### 5.2 Intra-Training Nutrition

| Session Duration | Need Supplementation? | Plan |
|:-----------------|:----------------------|:-----|
| < 45 minutes | No, just water | 150-200ml water every 15 minutes |
| 45-75 minutes | Not needed for low intensity / optional for high intensity | Sports drink 300-500ml/hour |
| > 75 minutes | Required | 30-60g carbs/hour + electrolytes |

**Sports drink recipe (make your own — cheaper and better):**
```
1L water
0.5-1g salt (adjust by sweat rate; more if hot)
30-60g honey or maltodextrin (adjust by session duration)
A splash of lemon juice (flavor + vitamin C)
```

**Why make your own instead of buying:**
- Commercial sports drinks are typically 6-8% carb concentration, which can be too concentrated and impair water absorption
- You can adjust the salt-to-sugar ratio based on your sweat rate and duration
- 10× cheaper

### 5.3 Post-Training Nutrition: The Golden Window

**Facts vs misconceptions:**
- "30-minute golden window": For amateur athletes training once a day, the window is 1-2 hours, not 30 minutes
- "Drink protein shake immediately": If you had a full meal 2 hours before training, replenishing carbs within 1 hour post-training is more important
- Who really needs to pay attention to the window: those training twice a day, in a bulking phase, or training fasted

**What to replenish post-training:**

| Component | Amount | Role | Sources |
|:----------|:------:|:-----|:--------|
| Fast carbs | 1-1.2g/kg bodyweight | Replenish muscle glycogen | White rice, potato, white bread, honey |
| Fast protein | 0.3-0.4g/kg bodyweight | Initiate muscle repair | Whey protein, egg whites, lean meat |
| Fluid | Weight lost × 1.5 | Rehydrate | Water / electrolyte water |

**70kg post-training calculation:**
- Carbs: 70 × 1.2 = 84g ≈ 2 bowls of white rice
- Protein: 70 × 0.4 = 28g ≈ 1 scoop whey + 1 egg
- Fluid: If you lost 0.5kg during training → 0.5 × 1.5 = 750ml

## Chapter 6: Periodization for Fat Loss / Muscle Gain

### 6.1 Fine-Tuning Nutrition During Fat Loss

Fat loss isn't "just eat less" — the goal is to lose fat, not muscle.

**Protein during fat loss: increase, not decrease**
```
Normal: 1.6g/kg
Fat loss: 2.0-2.4g/kg (protects muscle from being broken down)
```

**Carbs during fat loss: cut from around training**
```
Do NOT cut: pre- and post-training carbs (these maintain training quality)
Cut: pre-bed carbs and rest-day carbs
Start by cutting 200kcal from carbs and try for a week; if weight doesn't drop, cut another 200
```

**Monitoring metrics during fat loss:**
- Weigh in once a week (same time, same conditions)
- Measure waist circumference once a week
- Check whether strength levels are dropping (drop too fast → cutting too aggressively or not enough protein)
- Check whether post-training recovery has worsened

### 6.2 Key Points for Muscle Gain Nutrition

| Key Point | Explanation |
|:----------|:------------|
| A 200-500kcal surplus is enough | Above +500kcal/day, most of what you gain is fat, not muscle |
| Each kg of muscle gained requires ~5000-7000 extra kcal | So gaining 0.5-1kg of pure muscle per month is already excellent |
| More protein is not always better | Above 2.2g/kg, additional protein yields diminishing returns |
| Pre-bed protein matters | Growth hormone peaks at night; 20-30g casein before bed |

## Chapter 7: Hydration — Precise Calculation

### 7.1 Daily Hydration Formula

```
Baseline: body weight(kg) × 33ml
Training add: session duration(min) × 12ml
Heat add: ambient temp >30°C → +20%
```

**70kg athlete for one day:**
- Baseline: 70 × 33 = 2310ml
- 60-minute session: + 720ml
- Total: ~ 3000ml

### 7.2 Assessing Hydration Status

| Method | Standard |
|:-------|:---------|
| Urine color | Light lemon = good; dark yellow = dehydrated; clear = overhydrated |
| Thirst | By the time you feel thirsty, you're already 1-2% dehydrated — don't wait until thirsty |
| Pre- to post-training weight difference | Each 1kg lost → needs 1.5L water to replenish |
| Skin elasticity | Pinch skin on the back of your hand; check how fast it snaps back |

### 7.3 When to Supplement Electrolytes

| Condition | Need Electrolytes | Don't Need |
|:----------|:------------------|:-----------|
| Session duration | > 60 minutes high intensity | < 60 minutes |
| Sweat rate | Heavy sweating, clothes soaked | No sweat or light sweat |
| Ambient temperature | > 28°C | < 25°C |
| Multiple sessions/day | Needed after each session | Single session |

## Chapter 8: Common Supplements — Calculate Your Dose

### 8.1 Omega-3 (EPA + DHA)

**Who needs it:** Every athlete. Omega-3 is not optional — it's essential.

**Calculating your dose:**

| Purpose | Dose | 70kg Dose |
|:--------|:----:|:----------|
| Maintain neural health | 500mg EPA+DHA/day | Same |
| Anti-inflammatory / recovery | 1-2g EPA+DHA/day | Same |
| Therapeutic (under medical supervision) | 2-4g EPA+DHA/day | Follow doctor's order |

**⚠️ How to read labels:**
- A label saying "1000mg fish oil" does NOT mean 1000mg EPA+DHA
- Check the back: "EPA 300mg / DHA 200mg" → 500mg effective per capsule
- What matters is the total EPA+DHA, not the total fish oil

### 8.2 Magnesium

**Symptoms of magnesium deficiency (self-check):**

| Symptom | How It Relates to Magnesium |
|:--------|:----------------------------|
| Difficulty winding down before sleep | Magnesium regulates the parasympathetic nervous system |
| Muscles cramp easily | Magnesium participates in the contraction-relaxation cycle |
| Slow post-training recovery | Magnesium is involved in ATP synthesis |
| Poor sleep quality | Magnesium promotes deep sleep |
| 2 or more of these → likely magnesium deficient |

**Calculating your dose:**

| Type | Dose | When to Take | Notes |
|:-----|:----:|:-------------|:------|
| Maintenance | 200mg/day | Before bed | — |
| Training period | 300-400mg/day | Before bed | Sweating depletes magnesium |
| Correcting deficiency | 400-600mg/day | Split doses | Start low, increase gradually |

**Absorption rates of different magnesium forms:**
```
Magnesium glycinate: highest absorption, best for sleep ✅ First choice
Magnesium citrate: good absorption, has laxative effect ✓
Magnesium oxide: lowest absorption (~4%), cheap but poor efficacy ✗
Magnesium L-threonate: crosses the blood-brain barrier, most expensive, good for cognition
```

### 8.3 Vitamin D

**Why it matters for athletes:**
- 80% of people are vitamin D deficient (especially those training indoors)
- Vitamin D deficiency affects: muscle strength, recovery speed, immune function, bone density

**Calculating your dose:**

| Situation | Dose | Notes |
|:----------|:----:|:------|
| 15+ minutes outdoors daily | 1000 IU/day | Maintenance |
| Primarily indoor training (most people) | 2000-3000 IU/day | Supplement |
| Tested deficient | 5000+ IU/day | Follow doctor's order |
| Winter / northern latitudes | 3000-5000 IU/day | Cannot rely on sun exposure |

### 9.2 Nutrition Consultation Flow

```
Step 1 (5 min): Calculate TDEE
Ask: weight, height, age, gender, training frequency
Calculate: BMR → TDEE → goal-adjusted calories

Step 2 (5 min): Calculate the three macronutrients
Protein = body weight × factor
Fat = body weight × factor
Carbs = remaining calories ÷ 4

Step 3 (5 min): Distribution
How many meals per day? What time do you train? Earliest and latest eating times?
Distribute protein/carbs/fat per meal by time

Step 4 (5 min): Fine-tune
Body type adjustment → training day / rest day distinction → supplements needed?
```

### 9.3 Common Diet Problems — A Formulaic Approach

| Problem | The Answer Isn't "What to Eat" | It's Teaching "How to Think" |
|:--------|:-------------------------------|:-----------------------------|
| "No time to eat before training" | "Eat a banana" | "You need 0.3g/kg carbs within 30 minutes before training. Any portable carb works: banana = 25g carbs, energy bar = check the label" |
| "How to eat at the cafeteria / ordering delivery" | "Order a chicken salad" | "Check how much protein you still need, pick a main dish with that amount of protein, cut your rice portion in half, double the vegetables" |
| "Hungry during fat loss" | "Drink more water" | "Check if all your carbs are around training time. Put the largest carb meal right after training. If hungry before bed, have 30g protein (yogurt / casein)" |
| "Can't eat enough during bulking" | "Eat smaller, more frequent meals" | "Liquid meals (shakes / protein drinks). Don't exceed 0.5g/kg protein per meal (muscle protein synthesis saturates at this point)." |
| "What to eat on competition day" | "Have pasta 2 hours before" | "3 hours before: 1-2g/kg carbs (low GI). 1 hour before: 0.3g/kg carbs (moderate GI). During >60 min: 30g carbs/hour. Within 1 hour post: 1g/kg carbs + 0.3g/kg protein." |

## Chapter 10: Self-Assessment — How to Tell If Your Nutrition Plan Is Working

**Weekly checklist:**

| Metric | Good | Needs Adjustment |
|:-------|:----|:-----------------|
| Training performance | Strength/endurance increasing or maintained | Consistently declining |
| Recovery speed | Mostly recovered within 24 hours post-training | Still sore at 48 hours |
| Sleep quality | Fall asleep quickly, don't wake up, feel refreshed | Difficulty falling asleep, waking at night, tired upon waking |
| Weight change | Stable within ±0.5kg/week (maintenance phase) | Unexpected consistent loss or gain |
| How you feel | Energized during training, energetic daily | Persistent fatigue, low mood |

**When you see signals that need adjustment, troubleshoot in this order:**
1. Is total calorie intake sufficient? (Check day 1)
2. Is protein intake sufficient? (Check day 2)
3. Is carb timing correct? (Check day 3)
4. Is hydration adequate? (Easiest to overlook)
5. Check sleep quality and stress levels
