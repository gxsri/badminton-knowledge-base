# Chapter 1: China National Team

> ⚠️ **Reference note**: Based on publicly available information (Chinese Badminton Association, provincial sports bureau announcements, coach/player interviews in media). Specific standards vary by year, province, and coaching team.

## 1.1 Progression Path (Reference Framework)

```
L1: Sports School / Interest Class (ages 6-10)
  → 2-3x/week, 1.5hr/session
  → Cost: approx 50-100 RMB/session

→ Selection: City competition top 8 or coach recommendation

L2: Provincial Sports School (ages 10-14)
  → 5-6x/week, 2-3hr/session (morning classes, afternoon training)
  → Entry: selection test
  → Most costs covered by state

→ Selection: Provincial junior competition top 4 or trial match

L3: Provincial Team / National Youth Team (ages 14-17)
  → Full-time training, 6 days/week, 5-6hr/day
  → Entry: National Junior Championship top 8 or trial selection
  → Elimination rate: ~60% (technique/injury/psychology)

→ Selection: National Championship performance or trial matches

L4: National Team (ages 18+)
  → Full-time, national training center (Beijing/Hainan)
  → Entry: National Championship top 8 or trial matches
```

## 1.2 Training Philosophy: "Three Stricts + High Volume"

```
This is China's publicly stated training philosophy for competitive sports.

① Strict standards — movements must meet criteria, no compromise
② Difficult conditions — training difficulty > match difficulty
③ Match reality — everything serves actual match needs
④ High volume — quantity accumulates to quality

Training workflow example:
  Morning: Technique/footwork (~3hr)
  Afternoon: Conditioning/match (~3-4hr)
  Evening: Recovery/video review (as needed)
  Total varies by season (longest in off-season, shortest during competition)
```

## 1.3 Key Training Workflow: Four-Corner Multi-Shuttle

| Level | Speed | Ball interval | Sets | Per set |
|:------|:----:|:------------:|:----:|:-------:|
| L1 (beginner) | Slow | 3s | 3 | 10-15 |
| L2 (intermediate) | Medium | 2s | 4 | 20 |
| L3 (advanced) | Fast | 1.5s | 6 | 25-30 |
| L4 (pro) | Very fast | 1-1.2s | 8-10 | 30-40 |

## 1.4 What You Can Use from China's System

```
① Multi-shuttle training — the single most effective technique training method
② Video review — watch your own matches, find patterns
③ Internal competition — play against people who know your weaknesses
```

# Chapter 2: China National Youth Team

> ⚠️ **Reference note**: Based on general principles from China's publicly stated "Three Stricts" philosophy and publically available youth training guidelines.

## 2.1 Selection Process (Reference Framework)

```
Youth team selection is not an "exam" — it's "being seen."

General pathway:
  ① Perform well in provincial-level junior competitions
  ② Noticed by national youth team coaches at competitions
  ③ Invited to trial training camp (2-4 weeks)
  ④ Evaluation after camp: stay or return
```

**About test standards**:
- Specific numbers for jump rope, shuttle run, etc. are **NOT official published standards**
- Selection emphasizes: movement quality, learning ability, training attitude
- Reference: National U-series events (U12-14, U15-17) results are important indicators

## 2.2 Youth Training Core Concepts

```
Key differences between youth and senior training:

Concept ①: Foundation prioritizes CORRECTNESS over SPEED
  Youth training focuses on doing movements right before adding speed
  Method: break technique into 3-4 parts, master each before combining
  Common practice: slow down multi-shuttle so players have time for full motion

Concept ②: Training volume increases gradually, not at once
  Youth volume = ~60-70% of senior team
  10-15% increase per year gives body time to adapt
  Jumping directly to senior volume = high injury risk

Concept ③: Use constraints to promote thinking
  Restricted games are the most common youth method:
  - Only clears and nets (build rally patience)
  - Only backhand (build weak side)
  - Must finish point within 5 shots (build attack awareness)
```

## 2.3 Youth→Senior Transition Risks

```
Observed risks (based on published coach interviews citing ~50-70% elimination rate):

Risk ①: Physical adaptation — volume doubles overnight
  → Solution: first 4 weeks, actively limit volume to 80%

Risk ②: Psychological adaptation — from best to worst
  → Solution: don't look at rankings for first 6 months, only track personal progress

Risk ③: Self-management — from supervised to self-directed
  → Solution: build daily habit checklist (training, stretching, meals, sleep)
```

# Chapter 3: Japan — "Run Until You Can't" Philosophy

> ⚠️ **Reference note**: Based on publicly available information (Japan Badminton Association, corporate league public data, athlete interviews with Momota/Yamaguchi).

## 3.1 Core Training: 3-Person Rotation Multi-Shuttle

| Level | Work duration | Sets | Pace | HR zone |
|:------|:------------:|:----:|:----:|:-------:|
| L2 (beginner) | 20s×2 feeders=40s | 4 | 70% | 75-85% HRmax |
| L3 (intermediate) | 20s×3 feeders=60s | 6 | 85% | 85-90% HRmax |
| L4 (advanced) | 20s×3 feeders=60s | 8 | 100% | 90-95% HRmax |
| L5 (elite) | 25s×3 feeders=75s | 10 | 110% | 95%+ HRmax |

## 3.2 Kento Momota's "Instant Reaction" Training

```
Coach feeds flat/fast balls (not high clears)
Trainee stands at mid-court — minimize backswing
Target: ball-in to ball-out < 0.12 sec
Volume: 50 consecutive — speed cannot drop
```

## 3.3 What You Can Use from Japan's System

```
① Interval training based on actual match energy structure
② "Fast hands" training — shorten backswing, increase reaction speed
③ One-vs-two defensive training (building defensive limits)
```

# Chapter 4: Korea — Doubles Kingdom

> ⚠️ **Reference note**: Based on publicly available information (Korea Badminton Association, Park Joo-bong interviews, match video analysis).

## 4.1 Key Training: Half-Court Rotation

```
Setup: 2 players in one half court
Rules:
  Must rotate after each shot (no 2 consecutive by same player)
  Must complete attack→defense→attack transition
  
Purpose:
  Rotation awareness, position adjustment, non-verbal communication
```

## 4.2 Korean Mixed Doubles Philosophy

```
"Female players must have back-court capability."
  — Otherwise opponents just target the female player's backhand

Korean XD training includes female players doing same back-court volume as males.
```

## 4.3 What You Can Use from Korea's System

```
① Core strength focus — badminton power comes from torso rotation
② Rotation drills — practice switching positions without communication
③ Female back-court training — don't limit female players to net only
```

# Chapter 5: France — INSEP Model

> ⚠️ **Reference note**: Based on publicly available information (INSEP official website, French Badminton Association, player interviews).

## 5.1 French vs Asian Training Differences

| Dimension | China | Japan | Korea | France |
|:----------|:------|:------|:-------|:-------|
| Daily hours | 6-8hr | 5-7hr | 5-6hr | 3-4hr |
| Tech % | 50% | 40% | 45% | 70% |
| Fitness % | 35% | 45% | 40% | 20% |
| Youth system | Sports school | School+club | Mixed | INSEP (edu+training) |

## 5.2 French "Thinking Player" Training

```
After every 10 balls — stop and answer 3 questions:
  1. What did I do right?
  2. What did I do wrong?
  3. What will I change next set?

Cannot answer → that set was wasted.
Coach does not answer for you — you must learn self-diagnosis.
```

## 5.3 What You Can Use from France's System

```
① Multi-disciplinary approach — coach + physio + nutrition + psychology
② Self-diagnosis training — learn to identify your own problems
③ Quality over quantity — 50 focused reps > 500 sloppy reps
```

# Chapter 6: Methodology — Adapt to Your Environment

## 6.1 Environment Adaptation Formula

```
Your plan = Elite principle × Your resources ÷ Your constraints

Example 1: Office worker, 2x/week, no coach
  Training 1 (weekend): Technique 60min + Conditioning 30min
  Training 2 (weekday evening): Match play 60min + Technique 30min
  Morning (15min daily): Footwork shadow swings

Example 2: Coach with 10 students, 1 court
  Rotate groups: 3 students on court → 3 doing footwork → 3 doing strength
  15min rotations, 2hr session
```

## 6.2 Universal Training Design Formula

```
Training volume = Baseline × 60-70%
Progression condition: 2 consecutive sessions completed with good form
Progression increment: 5-10% per step
Deload condition: 2 consecutive sessions with declining quality → reduce 50%

These formulas work regardless of your level or environment.
```
