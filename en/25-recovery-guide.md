# Post-Training Recovery Guide

> **For**: All badminton players — training breaks you down, recovery builds you up. No recovery = no progress.  
> **Core idea**: Recovery doesn't start when you get home. It starts the moment you put your racket down.

---

## Chapter 1: The 24-Hour Recovery Timeline

### 1.1 The Golden Window (0-30 min after training)

```
① Cool-down (2-3 min)
  Light jog/walk. Bring heart rate below 100 bpm.
  Don't sit or lie down immediately — blood pools in your legs.

② Static stretching (5-8 min)
  Hold each 20-30 sec. Priority:
  Calves → Hamstrings → Hip flexors → Chest → Forearms

③ Nutrition (within 30 min)
  Fast carbs: 1-1.2g/kg bodyweight
  Fast protein: 0.3-0.4g/kg bodyweight
  Water: 1.5x weight lost during training
```

### 1.2 Sleep — The #1 Recovery Tool

```
Nothing substitutes for sleep.

Data:
  <6 hrs sleep → glycogen synthesis drops 30%
  <5 hrs sleep → cortisol rises → muscle breakdown
  3 consecutive nights <6 hrs → reaction time drops 15-20%

Recommendations:
  ☐ 7-9 hours per night
  ☐ Consistent sleep/wake schedule
  ☐ No screens 1 hour before bed
  ☐ Room temperature: 18-22°C
```

## Chapter 2: How to Know If You've Recovered

### 2.1 30-Second Morning Self-Check

```
① Resting heart rate: normal or elevated?
② Energy level: feel like moving? or feel heavy?
③ Sleep quality: woke up rested? or still tired?
④ Soreness: normal, significant, or sharp pain?

All normal → train normally
1-2 off → reduce volume
3+ off → rest day
Sharp pain → stop → [24-safety-guide](24-safety-guide.md)
```

### 2.2 Fatigue Accumulation Warning

```
If you feel "not recovered" for 3+ consecutive days →
your training volume exceeds your recovery capacity.
Solution: 2-3 days of complete rest, or 50% volume for one week.
```

## Chapter 3: Recovery Methods — What Works

```
Sleep: ⭐⭐⭐ Most important. 7-9 hrs. Non-negotiable.
Nutrition: ⭐⭐⭐ Within 30 min post-training. Carbs + protein.
Static stretch: ⭐⭐ Reduces soreness. Post-training.
Foam rolling: ⭐⭐ Reduces soreness. Post-training or rest days.
Cold therapy: ⭐⭐ For acute inflammation. First 48 hrs only.
Active recovery: ⭐⭐⭐ Light activity on rest days. Don't lie still.
```

## Chapter 4: Age-Specific Recovery

```
Recovery time varies significantly by age:

Under 25: Strength 24-48h, HIIT 24-36h, technique 4-6h
25-35: Strength 48-72h, HIIT 36-48h, technique 6-8h
35-45: Strength 72-96h, HIIT 48-72h, technique 8-12h
45+: Strength 96h+, HIIT 72h+, technique 12-24h

Key adjustments as you age:
① Extend warm-up (add 5 min per decade past 25)
② Reduce strength frequency, not quality
③ Prioritize sleep — it degrades with age
④ Listen to "I don't feel like training" — it's more meaningful at 40+
```

## Chapter 5: Mental Recovery

```
Physical fatigue recovers with sleep.
Mental fatigue doesn't.

Signs of mental fatigue:
  ☐ Checking phone during training
  ☐ No interest in training content
  ☐ Quality drops but body isn't tired
  ☐ Dreading training
  ☐ Easy things feel "hard"

Solutions:
① Change training content (don't force the same thing)
② Reduce volume, not frequency ("just warm up" → you'll likely train)
③ Find a training partner (best mental recovery tool)
④ Complete break (3-7 days no badminton at all)
```

## Chapter 6: Tournament Recovery

```
Between matches:
  □ Replenish carbs + protein (banana + whey protein is ideal)
  □ Change into dry clothes
  □ Quick foam rolling (5 min)
  □ Elevate legs for 10-15 min
  □ Don't nap longer than 20 min

After tournament ends:
  Day 0: nutrition + warm bath + early sleep
  Day 1: active recovery (walk/swim/cycle — light only)
  Day 2: assess → if recovered, light training
```

## Chapter 5: Weekly Recovery Plan Template

```
Mon: Train → stretch + nutrition + foam roll
Tue: Train → stretch + nutrition
Wed: Active recovery (walk 30min + foam roll)
Thu: Train → stretch + nutrition + foam roll
Fri: Train → stretch + nutrition
Sat: Match → nutrition + contrast bath + early sleep
Sun: Complete rest (no training, no "active recovery")
```

