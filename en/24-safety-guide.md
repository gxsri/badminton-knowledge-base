# Training Safety Guide

> **For**: All badminton players and coaches  
> **Core idea**: Injury is NOT part of training. Knowing when NOT to train is as important as knowing what to train.  
> **Reference**: NSCA Safety Guidelines, ACSM Exercise Contraindications

---

## Chapter 1: Pre-Training Self-Check — Can You Train Today?

### 1.1 Neck Check

```
Do this every morning (30 sec):

☐ Can you turn your head fully left and right?
☐ Can you touch your chin to your chest?
☐ Do you have a stiff neck + fever?

Any "no" → Don't train today. Rest 1-2 days.
```

### 1.2 Body Scan (1 min before each session)

```
① Any pain right now?
   □ No pain → train normally
   □ Sharp/stabbing pain → stop training that body part
   □ Normal soreness from yesterday → train but extend warm-up

② Energy level (1-10)?
   □ 5+ → train normally
   □ 3-4 → reduce volume or do recovery training
   □ 1-2 → rest today

③ Sleep last night?
   □ 7+ hrs → train normally
   □ 5-6 hrs → reduce volume
   □ <5 hrs → rest
```

## Chapter 2: Acute Injury — RICE Protocol

### 2.1 RICE — What to Do Immediately After Injury

```
Most common badminton acute injuries: ankle sprain, muscle strain, collision.

R — Rest
  Stop immediately. Don't "walk it off."
  Continuing will worsen the injury.

I — Ice
  Apply ice pack (wrapped in towel) for 15-20 min
  Every 2-3 hours for the first 48 hours
  NOT heat — ICE for the first 48 hours

C — Compression
  Wrap with elastic bandage
  Not too tight (if toes/fingers turn purple → too tight)
  Purpose: reduce swelling

E — Elevation
  Elevate injured area above heart level
  Purpose: use gravity to reduce swelling
```

### 2.2 When to See a Doctor

```
Go to the hospital immediately if:

☐ You heard a "pop" sound at the time of injury
☐ You cannot bear weight or move the injured area at all
☐ Swelling increases significantly within 30 minutes
☐ The injured area looks deformed
☐ Pain hasn't decreased after 24 hours
☐ You feel numbness or tingling
```

## Chapter 3: Red Flags — When You Must NOT Train

### 3.1 Red Alert — Stop Immediately

```
⚠️ Chest pain/tightness → possible cardiovascular issue, seek help
⚠️ Dizziness/vision blackout → brain not getting enough blood, sit down
⚠️ Joint "locks up" → possible meniscus/cartilage issue
⚠️ Same spot hurts for 3 consecutive sessions → don't "push through"
⚠️ Fever (>37.5°C) → exercising with fever can trigger myocarditis
```

### 3.2 Yellow Alert — Reduce Volume

```
🟡 Still sore from previous session (>72 hrs) → reduce 50%
🟡 Sleep deprivation (<5 hrs) → reduce or recovery only
🟡 Mild cold (runny nose/sore throat, no fever) → reduce 50%
  "Neck rule": symptoms above neck → light training OK
              symptoms below neck (chest/cough/body ache) → rest
```

## Chapter 4: Return-to-Play — 5-Level Protocol

### 4.1 The 5 Levels

```
After injury, do NOT skip any level.

Level 1: Pain-free daily activities (Days 1-3)
  ☐ Walking pain-free ☐ Stairs pain-free

Level 2: Pain-free light training (Days 4-7)
  ☐ Jogging pain-free ☐ Dynamic stretching pain-free

Level 3: Pain-free technique training (Days 7-14)
  ☐ 50% power shadow swings pain-free
  ☐ Fixed-position clears pain-free

Level 4: Pain-free full court training (Days 14-21)
  ☐ Full court footwork pain-free
  ☐ 70% intensity rally pain-free

Level 5: Return to match play (Day 21+)
  ☐ 100% intensity training pain-free
  ☐ No increased pain the day after training
```

### 4.2 The 24-Hour Rule

```
After each level, wait 24 hours:
  ☐ Pain not increased → can advance to next level
  ☐ Pain increased → go back one level, wait 2 more days
  ☐ Pain significantly worse → go back two levels or see a doctor
```
