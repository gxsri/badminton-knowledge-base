# Strength & Conditioning — Adaptive Guide

> **For whom**: Coaches and athletes
> **Core philosophy**: Strength training is not "do this exercise × 3 sets × 15 reps." It's about finding your 1RM, calculating your starting weight, deciding when to add and when to cut back.

---

## Chapter 1: Strength Training Isn't Just Lifting Weights

### 1.1 Types of Strength Required in Badminton

| Strength Type | Definition | When You Use It on Court | Training Method |
|:--------------|:-----------|:-------------------------|:----------------|
| **Maximal Strength** | The maximum force you can produce | During smashes | Heavy weight, low reps (1-5RM) |
| **Muscular Endurance** | How many times you can repeatedly produce sufficient force | Still running in the 3rd game | Moderate weight, high reps (15-25RM) |
| **Power** | How quickly you can produce force | Push-off starts, jumps | Light weight, explosive output |
| **Eccentric Control** | How well you can absorb force | Landing impact, directional braking | Slow lowering, fast lifting |
| **Stabilization Strength** | How well you can produce force while maintaining stability | Hitting while moving | Unilateral/unstable surface training |

### 1.2 Strength Training Focus by Stage

| Training Stage | Strength Priority | Why |
|:---------------|:------------------|:----|
| Level 0 (0-1 month) | Stabilization > Eccentric Control > Muscular Endurance | Learn to control joints first, then learn to exert force |
| Level 1 (1-3 months) | Muscular Endurance > Eccentric Control > Stabilization | You need to be able to repeat movements before building a foundation |
| Level 2+ (3+ months) | Power > Maximal Strength > Muscular Endurance | Technical improvements require a strength foundation to support them |

## Chapter 2: Preparation Before Training — Find Your Baseline First

### 2.1 Three Baseline Tests for Strength Training

No professional equipment needed — bodyweight is enough.

| Test | Method | What It Measures | Record Result |
|:-----|:-------|:-----------------|:--------------|
| **Squat Threshold** | Standard bodyweight squats until form breaks down; count reps | Lower body muscular endurance baseline | __ reps |
| **Push-up Threshold** | Standard push-ups (men) / kneeling push-ups (women) until form breaks down; count reps | Upper body pushing baseline | __ reps |
| **Row Threshold** | Band rows until form breaks down (scapula can no longer retract); count reps | Upper body pulling baseline | __ reps |

### 2.2 Calculating Starting Training Volume from Baseline

```
Starting volume formula = Baseline value × 60-70% (same formula as in 01-Core)

Example:
Squat threshold = 20 reps
Starting volume = 20 × 65% = 13 reps/set

Sets:
Baseline < 10 reps → 2 sets
Baseline 10-20 reps → 3 sets
Baseline > 20 reps → 4 sets (fewer reps, more sets)

Rest between sets:
Initial phase: 60-90 seconds (enough rest to ensure movement quality)
After progression: 30-60 seconds (increase metabolic stress)
```

### 2.3 Starting Volume Adjustment Coefficients by Age

| Age | Starting Volume Coefficient | Notes |
|:---:|:--------------------------:|:------|
| < 20 | 1.0 | Calculate directly from formula |
| 20-30 | 0.95 | Slight reduction |
| 30-40 | 0.85 | Recovery speed decreases, start conservatively |
| 40-50 | 0.75 | Connective tissue adapts more slowly |
| > 50 | 0.6 | Injury prevention is the top priority |

## Chapter 3: Shoulder Stabilization — Not "3 Sets a Day," But "Find Your Starting Resistance"

### 3.1 How to Choose Band Resistance

Not "use the blue band," but:

```
Step 1: Choose a resistance where you can complete 12-15 reps
Step 2: After completing 2 sets, do you feel a clear effort in the front of your shoulder?
  ● Yes → Resistance is appropriate
  ● Not at all → Resistance is too light, go heavier
  ● Shoulder shaking/pain after 2 sets → Resistance is too heavy, go lighter
Step 3: Record the band color / resistance level you used
```

### 3.2 Progression Criteria

```
You can add resistance/reps when:
1. You complete the target reps with good form for 3 consecutive training sessions
2. No shoulder discomfort (not soreness, but discomfort) 24 hours after training
3. Self-assessed "effort level" is below 7/10 (1=too easy, 10=maximum)

All 3 conditions met → You can progress
How to progress: First add reps (1-2 reps at a time). Once stable at 15 reps, then add resistance.
```

### 3.3 Shoulder Stabilization Training Cycle

| Phase | Duration | Training Content | How to Determine Moving to Next Phase |
|:------|:---------|:-----------------|:---------------------------------------|
| **Activation** | 2-4 weeks | Low-resistance band external/internal rotation, YTWL | Can complete 3 sets × 15 reps with proper form, no compensation |
| **Stabilization** | 4-6 weeks | Increase resistance, increase training frequency | Shoulder strength ratio (external rotation:internal rotation) approaches 2:3 |
| **Integration** | Ongoing | Shoulder training integrated into full-body training | — |

## Chapter 4: Knee Injury Prevention — Not "How Many Reps," But "Can You Do It"

### 4.1 The First Threshold for Knee Training

**Before any knee training, pass this test:**

```
Test: Stand on one leg, slowly squat to 45 degrees of knee bend, then slowly stand up
Standard: Knee tracks over the second toe throughout, no valgus (caving in), no wobbling

Result Interpretation:
☐ Can do it, no issues → Proceed to knee strengthening training
☐ Can do it but knee caves in → Do glute medius activation for 2 weeks, then retest
☐ Cannot do it (unstable/pain) → Need physical therapy, pause knee training
```

**If your knee caves in — don't train the leg, train the glutes.**

```
Glute Medius Activation (do daily until you pass the test):
1. Clamshells 2 sets × 15 reps (focus on feeling the glute engage, not the rep count)
2. Side-lying leg raises 2 sets × 15 reps (same as above)
3. Single-leg glute bridge 2 sets × 12 reps (drive through the stance leg's glute, not the lower back)

How to tell if activation worked:
After activation, redo the single-leg squat test —
Knee valgus reduced → Activation worked
Knee valgus unchanged → Activation is being done incorrectly (re-check form) or needs more time
```

### 4.2 Knee Training Volume Is Not Fixed

The variable in knee training is **quality of control**, not **quantity**.

| Control Quality | Your Performance | Appropriate Training Volume |
|:----------------|:-----------------|:---------------------------|
| Poor (requires full concentration to prevent valgus) | Can do 2-3 quality reps | Do 2-3 reps per set, more sets (4-6 sets), long rest |
| Fair (can control without full concentration) | Can do 5-8 quality reps | 3 sets × 5 reps |
| Good (can do it while talking) | Can do 10+ quality reps | 3 sets × 8-10 reps, can add load |

**Principle: Training volume for the knee is determined by control quality, not by "how many you should be able to do."**

## Chapter 5: Metabolic Conditioning — Not "Running Laps," But Calculating by Formula

### 5.1 Your Energy System Starting Training Volume

```
Step 1: Know your "anaerobic threshold"
  Test: High knees for 30 seconds → measure 10 sec heart rate → ×6 = heart rate per minute
  Your anaerobic threshold ≈ (220 - age) × 0.88
  
Step 2: Determine your training zone
  Aerobic training: HR = (220 - age) × 0.6-0.7
  Anaerobic training: HR = (220 - age) × 0.8-0.9
  
Step 3: Duration
  Aerobic start: 15 minutes (maintain this HR zone for 15 minutes)
  Interval start: 10 sec work + 50 sec rest × 6 rounds (1:5 ratio)
```

### 5.2 Core Variables of Interval Training

Interval training is not "how many sets, how much rest" — it's four adjustable variables:

| Variable | What It Adjusts | How to Adjust |
|:---------|:----------------|:--------------|
| **Work Intensity** | How hard you work during the work interval | From 80% to 95% |
| **Work Duration** | How long each work interval lasts | From 10 seconds to 60 seconds |
| **Rest Duration** | How long you rest | From 1:5 to 1:1 |
| **Total Rounds** | Total number of rounds | From 4 rounds to 12 rounds |

**Beginners (Level 0-1): Adjust only one variable at a time — time.**
- Week 1: 10 sec work / 50 sec rest × 4 rounds
- Week 2: 10 sec work / 50 sec rest × 6 rounds (only adjust total rounds)
- Week 3: 15 sec work / 50 sec rest × 6 rounds (only adjust work duration)
- Week 4: 15 sec work / 45 sec rest × 6 rounds (only adjust rest duration)

## Chapter 6: Training Periodization — Not Giving You a 12-Week Plan

### 6.1 The Core Decision Tree for Periodic Training

```
You've done 2-3 weeks of strength training —

Ask yourself: Is strength increasing or stagnating?
  ● Increasing → Continue current approach
  ● Stagnating → Move to next step

Ask yourself: Can you recover 48 hours after training?
  ● Yes → Try increasing volume (add sets/reps)
  ● No → Deload for one week (50% training volume) then come back

Ask yourself: Is your strength translating to technical training?
  ● Yes → Continue strength training
  ● No → Adjust strength training movement patterns to better match technical movements

Ask yourself: Is there any pain?
  ● No → Train normally
  ● Yes (same spot hurts for 3 consecutive training sessions) → Stop that exercise, substitute with an alternative
```

### 6.2 A 4-Week Strength Training Cycle Template (Not a Fixed Plan)

```
【Week 1】Adaptation
Goal: Learn the movement, build neural connections
Volume: 70% of starting volume
Intensity: Light
Feeling: Should feel "too easy," not "this is hard"

【Week 2】Foundation
Goal: Stabilize the movement, increase muscular endurance
Volume: 85% of starting volume
Intensity: Light-to-moderate
Feeling: Slightly challenging, but doable

【Week 3】Development
Goal: Increase strength
Volume: 100% of starting volume
Intensity: Moderate
Feeling: Challenging, last 2-3 reps require effort

【Week 4】Consolidation/Deload
Goal: Consolidate gains, recover
Volume: 50% of starting volume
Intensity: Light-to-moderate
Feeling: Easy, recharge for the next cycle

Note: This is not a calendar. If doing 70% in week 1 already feels unstable,
stay at 70% until movement quality improves. Do not increase volume just to "follow the plan."
```

### 6.3 Diagnostic Tree for Strength Training Problems

| Problem You Encounter | First Step to Check | Second Step |
|:----------------------|:--------------------|:------------|
| Strength increasing but technique not improving | Are the strength training movements similar to your technical movements? | Add sport-specific movement pattern training into strength work (e.g., banded clear simulations) |
| Strength increasing but getting injured easily | Are you only training "mirror muscles"? | Check front-to-back strength ratios and left-right strength imbalances |
| Strength plateaued for a month | Has training volume stayed the same? | Add sets → Add reps → Add resistance (in that order) |
| Strength decreasing | Is it accumulated fatigue? | Rest one week or deload to 50%, then reassess |
| Prolonged soreness after training (>72 hours) | Did you increase volume too quickly? | Go back to 80% of the last volume you could complete and recover from |
