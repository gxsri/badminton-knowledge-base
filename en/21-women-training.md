> **Core idea**: Women are not "smaller men." Training must adapt to female physiology.

# Chapter 1: Key Physiological Differences

| Metric | Male | Female |
|:-------|:----:|:------:|
| Muscle mass | 35-45% | 25-35% |
| Normal body fat | 10-20% | 20-30% |
| Max strength | baseline | 60-75% of male |
| Lower body strength | baseline | 70-80% of male |
| Upper body strength | baseline | 50-60% of male |
| Hemoglobin | 13-17g/dL | 12-15g/dL |
| Aerobic capacity | baseline | 85-95% of male |

**Training implications**:
① Upper body needs targeted training (not just reduced volume)
② Lower body can be trained at similar intensity
③ Aerobic capacity gap is small — train at same intensity

# Chapter 2: Menstrual Cycle Training (4 Phases)

## Phase ①: Menstruation (Days 1-5) — Low Intensity

```
Energy lowest, recovery slowest.

Option A (light training, if feeling OK):
  Total: 45-60 min
  Technique only (no explosive movement)
  Sets: 15 reps × 3 sets, rest 90s
  No heavy strength, no HIIT

Option B (complete rest):
  Valid choice, especially with heavy flow
  No guilt needed — rest IS training
```

## Phase ②: Follicular Phase (Days 6-14) — Peak Performance

```
Strength ↑, power ↑, recovery fastest.
This is your 10-day training window.

Training example:
  Strength: 3 sets × 8 reps at 80-85% 1RM
  Technique: smash/drop — 20 reps × 4 sets, rest 60s
  Conditioning: 15s work / 15s rest × 12 rounds
```

## Phase ③: Ovulation (Days 15-17) — High Intensity, Watch ACL Risk

```
⚠️ ACL injury risk is 3-4x higher (estrogen loosens ligaments).

Mandatory landing control training FIRST:
  - Jump → land hold 3s stable: 10 reps
  - Side jump → land hold: 10 reps/side
  - Single-leg landing: knee over toes × 10/side

Avoid: explosive cutting when fatigued
```

## Phase ④: Luteal Phase (Days 18-28) — Lower Volume, Maintain Quality

```
Body temp ↑0.3-0.5°C, heart rate 5-10bpm higher at same intensity.

Not suitable: heavy strength (drops 5-10%), long HIIT sessions
Suitable: technique refinement, tactical training, moderate aerobic (60-70% HRmax)
Extend warm-up by 5 min
```

# Chapter 3: Injury Prevention — ACL Focus

```
Women have 4-6x higher ACL injury rate than men (NSCA data).

Prevention routine (2-3x/week, 10 min):
  ① Glute med activation: clam shells 15/side × 2
  ② Landing control: jump → hold 3s × 10
  ③ Hamstring strength: nordic curl × 8 × 2
  ④ Cutting control: slow → fast, knee over toes
```

# Chapter 4: Coaching Female Athletes

```
Research shows (NSCA Female Athlete Training):
  Male athletes focus on RESULTS ("my smash speed")
  Female athletes focus on FEELING ("did I do it right")

So:
  To a male: "Your smash speed increased 5 km/h."
  To a female: "Your kinetic chain sequence was smooth today."
```

# Chapter 5: Common Myths

```
Myth ①: "Women can't train during menstruation"
  Fact: Light training is fine and may reduce pain. Listen to your body.

Myth ②: "Women's training should be 70% of men's"
  Fact: Lower body can be equal intensity. Upper body needs adjusted content, not just reduced.

Myth ③: "Strength training makes women bulky"
  Fact: Women have 1/10-1/20 of men's testosterone. Strength training builds capability, not bulk.
```
