# Match Strategy — A Systematic Practical Guide

> **Who this is for**: Players with match experience who often finish games thinking "I don't know where I lost", "I blew the lead", or "my energy distribution was off"
> **Prerequisites**: Level 1 technical training completed; currently playing or preparing to compete
> **Core philosophy**: A match is not "go out and play" — it's "execute a pre-prepared strategy system"

## Chapter 1: Energy/Stamina Distribution — Not "Saving Energy", It's "Calculating Energy"

### 1.1 Match Energy Budget

Most people allocate energy during a match based on feel: if they still feel strong they go all out, if they feel tired they hold back.

The right approach: **Calculate your energy budget before the match.**

```
Your available total energy = how many minutes you can sustain high-intensity play

How to measure:
Pick a day and play one complete 21-point game (go all out)
Record the point at which you first feel noticeably fatigued

Example: You start feeling noticeably tired at point 12
→ Your high-intensity energy can sustain about 12-15 points
→ A three-game 21-point match has about 60-80 points → You need to distribute energy across three games
```

### 1.2 Three-Game Energy Distribution Formula

```
Game 1: Use 40% of your total energy
  Purpose: Establish rhythm, gather information (not go all out)
  Strategy: Control-oriented play; don't chase every kill shot

Game 2: Use 35%
  If you won Game 1: Maintain rhythm, don't ease up
  If you lost Game 1: Determine whether it was a physical issue or a technical/tactical issue
    - Physical issue → Conserve energy in Game 2, save for Game 3
    - Technical/tactical issue → Adjust strategy in Game 2

Game 3: Use the remaining 25% + willpower
  Everyone is out of energy by Game 3.
  Winning or losing Game 3 doesn't depend on who has better stamina —
  it depends on who can do one more thing right when they're exhausted.
```

### 1.3 Between-Point Recovery (Not Just Standing Around)

| Situation | What To Do | Heart Rate Recovery |
|:----------|:-----------|:-------------------:|
| After a long rally (>10 shots) | 2 deep breaths + slow walk back | Drop 10-15bpm |
| Won a short rally (<3 shots) | Normal breathing, maintain focus | Drop 5bpm |
| Lost a short rally (<3 shots) | 1 deep breath + self-talk "next point" | Drop 5-10bpm |
| Interval break (60 seconds) | Drink water + towel off + sit 30s + strategy review 20s + get back on court | Drop 20-30bpm |

**Formula:**
```
Heart rate recovery after a point, before the next serve ≤ resting heart rate + 30bpm
If you hit this range → your energy distribution is reasonable
If you can't recover to this range → that point cost too much — you're overexerting or your shot efficiency is low
```

### 1.4 Common Energy Distribution Mistakes

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|:--------|:--------|:----|
| Opening too hot | Sprinting flat-out in the first half of Game 1, cliff-drop in the second half | Use only 70% speed for the first 5 points of Game 1 |
| Giving up after losing Game 1 | Conserving energy in Game 2, abandoning tactics | It's a three-game format; Game 1 is just probing — the real match starts in Game 2 |
| Suddenly going all out on critical points | Fatigue from earlier points erupts on big points | Don't suddenly change pace on critical points — use your most stable play |
| Not drinking water after a game | Dehydration in Game 3 leads to cramps/dizziness | Must drink water between every game, even if you're not thirsty |

## Chapter 2: Probing Shots — The First 5 Points Aren't for Scoring

### 2.1 The Only Goal of the First 5 Points: Gather Information

```
In the first 5 points, you should NOT think "how do I win this point."
You should think "what information about my opponent am I learning from this point?"
```

### 2.2 Probing Checklist (Test in Order Over the First 5 Points)

```
Point 1: Test forehand baseline
  Hit a high-quality forehand clear to the baseline
  Observe: opponent's return quality, their position after the return, forehand technique consistency

Point 2: Test backhand baseline
  Hit a high-quality backhand clear to the baseline (or to their backhand side)
  Observe: does the opponent transition or attack from backhand? Do they lose position easily after a backhand return?

Point 3: Test net play
  Play a net shot
  Observe: their speed moving forward, net feel, do they recover or stay at the net?

Point 4: Test defensive/passive shot handling
  Play a shot that forces them to run defensively (net + rearcourt movement)
  Observe: their shot selection when under pressure (always the same direction? or random?)

Point 5: Test smash defense
  Find a chance to smash
  Observe: their ability to receive the smash, the quality and placement of their return

After 5 points, you should already know:
1. Which is weaker — their forehand or backhand
2. Their movement ability
3. Whether their net or rearcourt is stronger
4. Which direction they most often hit when under pressure
5. Their approximate stamina level
```

### 2.3 Applying Probing Results

| What You Discover | Corresponding Match Strategy |
|:------------------|:-----------------------------|
| Backhand return quality is poor | Send 70% of shots to the backhand baseline |
| Net feel is poor | Play more net shots, force them to lift |
| Poor stamina | More rallies and drives, don't chase one-shot kills |
| Always hits straight when pressured | After making them run, slightly favor the straight side |
| Weak smash defense | Smash whenever you have a chance |
| Can return smashes but placement is poor | Smash then immediately follow to the net to cut off the return |

## Chapter 3: Weakness Analysis — Find It Systematically, Not "I Feel Like He's Weak Here"

### 3.1 Four Types of Weaknesses

Not all weaknesses are the same. Different types require different responses.

| Weakness Type | Definition | Signs | How To Exploit |
|:--------------|:-----------|:------|:---------------|
| **Technical Weakness** | A specific technique is inconsistent | Backhand return is short, smashes often go out | Keep hitting to that technical point until they make an error |
| **Movement Weakness** | Slow in a particular direction | Slow backward/sideways movement, uneven lateral speed | Hit more in that direction, make them move frequently |
| **Decision Weakness** | Makes bad choices in specific situations | Always hits the same direction when pressured, hesitates on big points | Create that situation and wait for them to make a mistake |
| **Psychological Weakness** | Performance drops under specific pressure | Error rate rises on big points, rushes when being caught up | Create that pressure scenario, don't give them breathing room |

### 3.2 Weakness Discovery System (No Coach Needed — You Can Use It Yourself)

```
After each game, take 2 minutes to fill out:

Opponent's:
Technical Weakness: __________(be specific about which technique)
  Evidence: He returned ___ backhand shots, ___ of which were short

Movement Weakness: __________(be specific about which direction)
  Evidence: I hit to his ___ direction ___ times, he was late ___ times

Decision Weakness: __________(be specific about which situation)
  Evidence: He was pressured ___ times and hit the same direction ___ times

Psychological Weakness: __________(be specific about which pressure)
  Evidence: He made ___ errors on critical points and ___ errors on normal points
```

### 3.3 Shot Combinations for Exploiting Weaknesses

```
After finding a weakness, don't just "attack that one spot."
The opponent will adjust.

The right approach: Use shot combinations to lock in on their weakness.

Example: Opponent's backhand is weak

Wrong approach: Hit every shot to the backhand baseline
→ Opponent adapts, shifts their stance toward the backhand, opening up the forehand gap

Right approach:
Shot 1: Backhand baseline → their return quality is poor
Shot 2: Forehand baseline → they just ran from the backhand, now their forehand is also defensive
Shot 3: Backhand baseline again → they run back again, and this shot they'll most likely make an error

Principle: It's not "repeatedly attack their weakness" — it's "make them burn energy defending their weakness, then face that weakness again while fatigued."
```

## Chapter 4: Targeted Training Methodology — Bring Match Problems Back to Training

### 4.1 Match-Training Loop

```
Match → Identify Problem → Design Targeted Training → Solve in Training → Verify in Next Match

The step most people skip is "Design Targeted Training."

Most players finish a match, find a problem, then train the same thing the following week.
Result: the same problem shows up in the next match again.
```

### 4.2 From Match Problem to Training Plan

| Problem In The Match | What NOT To Train | What To Train Instead |
|:---------------------|:------------------|:----------------------|
| "My backhand is always short" | Train backhand technique | First train the footwork path to the backhand, then determine if it's a footwork issue or a technique issue |
| "I can't run in Game 3" | Train stamina | First check if Game 1 was too intense (energy distribution issue), then check if training ever simulated Game 3 intensity |
| "I keep making errors on big points" | Train technique | First check what shots you're playing on big points — are you choosing an unstable shot? |
| "Lost to the same person twice" | Train technique | First analyze whether you lost for the same reason both times — if yes, your targeted training was wrong |

### 4.3 Targeted Training Design Formula

```
Targeted Training = Your Problem × Match Simulation Level

Problem weight:
  Problem appears every match → training allocation 40%
  Problem appears occasionally → training allocation 20%
  Problem doesn't affect results → training allocation 10%

Match simulation level:
  The closer the training content is to match conditions → the better the result
  Levels:
    L1: Pressure-free repetition (technique correction)
    L2: Scored but no consequences (technique verification)
    L3: Scored + consequences (pressure training)
    L4: Full match conditions (match simulation)
```

**Example:**

```
Problem: Stamina drop in Game 3 leads to technique breakdown
Problem weight: High (40%)
Match simulation level needed: L3-L4

Training plan:
1. First do stamina training (intervals/footwork circuits) until fatigued (simulating Game 3 energy level)
2. Immediately do technical training while fatigued (simulating in-match technical execution)
3. Score points, with error penalties
4. Record error rate while fatigued
5. When error rate while fatigued approaches error rate at normal energy → problem solved
```

### 4.4 Match Video Analysis Method (Using Video Instead of a Coach)

```
If you can record your match video —

Order to watch (NOT from start to finish):

Step 1: Watch only your serves and serve receives (5 minutes)
  Your serve: Do you have a fixed service habit? Is the opponent reading your serve?
  Your serve receive: Where do you return the serve? Did the opponent anticipate it?

Step 2: Watch only the points you lost (10 minutes)
  For every point lost:
  1. Was it a technical error or did the opponent just play well?
  2. If technical error: was it a technique issue or a decision issue?
  3. If the opponent played well: was it a positioning issue or did they genuinely hit an unreturnable shot?

Step 3: Watch only the long rallies (10 minutes)
  Rallies of 5+ shots:
  1. Who made the first error?
  2. What happened in the 3 shots before the error?
  3. Do your shots in long rallies follow a pattern?

Step 4: Summary (5 minutes)
  3 things done well: _____
  3 things to improve: _____
  Adjustment for next match: _____
```

### 4.5 Pre-Match Targeted Training Plan (One Week Example)

```
Assume you're playing a known opponent next weekend.

Monday: Review records from your last match against this opponent
  Their strongest technique: _____
  Their weakest technique: _____
  Where I lost last time: _____

Tuesday: Targeted technical training (40% of training time)
  Design shot combinations targeting their weakness
  Example: their backhand is weak → practice the "forehand baseline → backhand baseline" combination

Wednesday: Stamina + simulation training (30% of training time)
  Practice your match strategy while fatigued

Thursday: Rest

Friday: Tactical drills (30% of training time)
  Find a practice partner with a similar style, simulate match conditions

Saturday: Pre-match adjustment
  Light training + check equipment + confirm strategy

Sunday: Match
  Execute strategy → gather information → post-match record
```

## Chapter 5: In-Match Real-Time Adjustments — Not "Stick to the Plan No Matter What"

### 5.1 When to Adjust Your Strategy

```
Before the match, you set a strategy.
But during the match, you need to check every 5 points whether the strategy is still working.

Check method:

If losing consecutive points (3+):
  Ask yourself: Am I not executing well, or is the strategy itself wrong?
    - Not executing well → Go back to basics, play the simplest shots
    - Strategy is wrong → Switch strategy

If the opponent has adjusted:
  They changed their style → You must change too
  Example: You've been attacking their backhand, now they're favoring the backhand side → attack the forehand

If you're already leading (>5 points):
  Don't change strategy. Changing strategy while leading is the most common reason for blowing a lead.
```

### 5.2 Strategy Switch Decision Tree

```
You've lost 3 consecutive points —

First question: Can my stamina still support my current style of play?
  No → Slow down the pace, play more clears to buy recovery time
  Yes → Go to the second question

Second question: Is my execution the problem?
  Yes (too many errors) → Lower risk, play shots you're confident in
  No (shots are well-placed but he's returning them) → Go to the third question

Third question: Has he adapted to my style?
  Yes → Switch to a shot pattern or pace he hasn't adapted to yet
  No → Continue current strategy, add more patience

Fourth question: Am I making the right decision in key situations?
  No → Tell yourself clearly "next time this situation comes up, I hit it here"
  Yes → Keep going, trust yourself
```

### 5.3 Different Approaches When Leading and Trailing

| Situation | Mindset | Play Style | Taboos |
|:----------|:--------|:-----------|:-------|
| **Leading by 5+ points** | Stay focused, don't ease up | Keep executing what got you the lead | Suddenly changing play style, trying new shots |
| **Leading by 3-5 points** | Control the pace, don't chase kill shots | Play controlled shots, reduce forced attacks | Rushing to end the game, playing risky shots |
| **Tied** | Initiate changes, break the balance | Play a shot pattern you haven't used yet | Falling into the opponent's rhythm |
| **Trailing by 3-5 points** | Adjust, don't collapse | Change pace (fast→slow or slow→fast) | Keep playing the same pattern (opponent has already adapted) |
| **Trailing by 5+ points** | Take risks | Play every shot that "is unstable but can score" | Giving up (the match isn't over until it's over) |

## Chapter 6: Building Your Match Strategy Database

### 6.1 Strategy Templates for Different Opponents

```
Opponent A (Defensive, good stamina)
  Strategy: Play more net shots + don't chase one-shot kills
  Taboo: Getting into long rallies with them

Opponent B (Aggressive, high error rate)
  Strategy: Give them lots of shots to play, wait for errors
  Taboo: Trading attacks with them

Opponent C (Technical, good net play)
  Strategy: Play more rearcourt shots, keep them away from the net
  Taboo: Battling them at the net

Opponent D (Stamina-based, fast runner)
  Strategy: Use tactical variation, don't compete on stamina
  Taboo: Getting into a driving/rallying contest with them
```

Every time you play a new opponent, record their characteristics and your strategy. Next time you meet them, you won't need to spend 5 points probing.

### 6.2 What Your "Match Notebook" Should Record

```
Match Date: _____ Opponent: _____

Basic Information:
  Opponent Style: _____ (Aggressive/Defensive/Technical/Stamina)
  Score: ____:____

Pre-Match Strategy: _____
Execution: _____ (Fully executed / Partially executed / Not executed at all)

In-Match Adjustments:
  What was adjusted and when: _____

Post-Match Summary:
  Opponent's Weaknesses: _____
  My Problems: _____
  What to Improve Next Time: _____
```
